What to notice

  • The dashed EAS curve ends at the same lift-off speed everywhere (85.49 m/s at 79 t): the pilot sees the same indicated speed. The true speed, and so the ground speed, is higher by 1/√σ.
  • Density enters twice: the wing (s ∝ 1/ρ∞) and the engines (T ∝ σ0.75). At 0.7 VLO the dynamic pressure is the same at every field, so D, L and μr(W − L) do not change with altitude: the net force falls faster than the thrust.
  • The lecture formula (forces at 0.7 VLO) and the integrated roll agree within about 1 %: the 0.7 VLO average is a very good one.
  • On a warm La Paz afternoon the main lever is weight: every +1 °C costs about 229 kg. A headwind or an early-morning (cooler) departure also helps.

Model: ISA troposphere, temperature Θ (K; T means thrust), off-standard day ρ = ρISA·ΘISA/(ΘISA+ΔΘ) (same pressure). VLO = 1.2 Vstall. Lecture formula (primary): sLO = 1.44 W²/(g ρ∞ S cl,max [T − (D + μr(W − L))]avg) with L = q S cl, D = q S [cd,0 + φ cl²/(π e AR)], φ = (16h/b)²/(1+(16h/b)²), AR = b²/S, forces at 0.7 VLO, cl = 0.8 in the roll; thrust constant, T = 0.85 · TSL · σ0.75. Check: m dV/dt = T − D − μr(W − L) integrated with RK4, Δt = 0.01 s (the V(s) curves). Classroom field-length rule (lecture formula): 1.15 × sLO must fit on the runway (a stand-in for certified take-off distance with engine-out cases). Maximum masses are rounded down to 10 kg (conservative); the "weight-limited" preset loads that mass. Aircraft data are 737-800-class and representative.
